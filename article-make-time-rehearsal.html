Make Time for Rehearsal

PREV_INSIGHT
Professional Tips · 5 min read · By Doug Kunnath · April 2026

The keynote walks on. The screen says "No Signal." The room goes quiet in the wrong way.

That failure almost always had a rehearsal slot that got cut for "we don't have time." Rehearsal is the cheapest insurance on the schedule.

Three useful kinds

  • Tech check: Inputs, clickers, playback, wireless mics, confidence monitors — advance every slide once
  • Cue-to-cue: Walk the timed moments (walk-in music out, mic handoff, video roll, lights up)
  • Dress / full run: Speakers on their feet with the real clock, even if you only run the critical segments

What to protect on the run-of-show

Block time and treat it like load-in: non-negotiable. A short cue-to-cue beats a vague "we'll test in the morning" that collides with floral and catering.

Mistakes we still see

  • Rehearsing on a different laptop than show day
  • Skipping the first-slide and last-slide checks
  • No mic handoff practice between host and speakers
  • Videos untested with the house audio path

By event type

Wedding: Ceremony mics and processional levels. Gala: Ask video and spotlight cues. Conference: Switcher rehearsal between breakouts and plenary.

If something is going to break, you want it to break at 14:00 with an empty house — not at 19:12 with donors in their seats.
