How to Build a Presentation That Holds Attention

PREV_INSIGHT
Corporate Events · 6 min read · By Doug Kunnath · February 2025

Most presentations don't fail because the slides are ugly. They fail because the speaker is reading the slides, the clicker dies at minute four, and nobody checked whether the laptop would sleep mid-deck.

I've sat FOH for a lot of talks — boardrooms, ballrooms, museum halls. The ones that hold a room share a few boring habits. Here's what actually helps.

Open with something the room can use

Your first thirty seconds should not be a title slide and an apology about the clicker. Give people a reason to stay: a concrete problem, a short story with a point, or one number that matters to this audience. Save the agenda for slide two.

Fewer slides, bigger type

If the people in the back can't read it without squinting, it isn't a slide — it's a handout. Aim for one idea per slide, 24pt minimum on body text, and enough empty space that the eye knows where to look. Dark text on light (or the reverse) beats brand gradients that wash out under house lights.

Talk to people, not to the screen

Face the room. Pause after the line that matters. Walk if it helps; don't pace like you're lost. If you need notes, put them on a confidence monitor or printed cards — not in six-point type on the live slide.

Build in one moment of contact

A show of hands, a two-minute pair discussion, or a single live poll is enough. You're not running a workshop. You're interrupting the phone-checking reflex.

Rehearse with the real kit

Run the deck on the actual laptop, clicker, and switcher. Advance every slide once. Check video embeds and fonts. The "it worked at home" failure mode is still the most common one we see.

What we watch from the back

  • Are phones up or faces forward?
  • Did the Q&A actually start, or did people bolt?
  • Can someone repeat your three points afterward?

Content, delivery, and the room setup have to agree. Fix any one of them and the talk gets better. Fix all three and people stay until the end.
